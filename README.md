# Why we are here?
I believe that designers, as “outsiders” to coding, do not need to understand programming as a professional developer would. However, we should have a basic understanding of its underlying logic and understand how coding can function within a design project.

One reason designers should learn coding is that, it can become a tool for discovering, expressing, and understanding art. For example, learning to code allows us to understand about something like John Conway’s Game of Life in a more direct way. Coding is not simply a technical skill; it can also become a medium through which we discover new forms of artistic expression. One Chinese phrase I particularly like is “文章本天成，妙手偶得之,” I think new tools often provide precisely these unexpected opportunities for discovery. As Dylan Beattie discusses in The Art of Code, computers have enabled entirely new forms of art, such as “Deep Dreaming.” As technology continues to develop, we will discover more forms of artistic expression that were previously unimaginable.

Many people enjoy H.P. Lovecraft’s work for the idea that “the oldest and strongest emotion of mankind is fear of the unknown.” However, my favorite idea associated with Lovecraft is: that the terrifying truth emerges when knowledge from different disciplines is connected. Although this idea serves to articulate the construction of a horror narrative, I think it applies to art. I believe that the connection and collision between different disciplines are important conditions for the creation of interesting and sophisticated art. Each discipline has its own form of aesthetic value, and when these different forms of knowledge interact, they can produce something that neither discipline could have created independently. This is my second reason for learning to code as a designer. I believe that the process of programming itself can also be enjoyable, and enjoyment, in a broader sense, is an important condition for experiencing and creating aesthetics.

Finally, nothing can develop independently of the environment in which it exists, art and design are no exception. We are living in an era of rapid technological development. Even professions that may seem to have little connection with technology, such as sanitation work, are increasingly supported by technological products and systems. Completing a complex project often requires collaboration between people from multiple disciplines. Designers naturally become part of this process. Designers need to understand how programs work in order to collaborate more effectively with programmers and ensure the project is executed comprehensively and efficiently.

In conclusion, I believe that coding is a tool for discovering, understanding, and creating art. Designers need to understand technology so that they can communicate and collaborate with programmers and effectively bring their designs into reality. At the same time, learning to code allows us to experience the joy and aesthetic possibilities that emerge from the collision between different disciplines. In an age when AI can generate code on demand, perhaps learning coding is no longer primarily about learning how to write every line of code ourselves. Instead, it is about understanding what code can do, discovering what becomes possible through it, and finding our own ways to create with it. This is why we are here.
